About

I'm a PhD student in Biological Anthropology at the University of Cambridge, supervised by Dr Sylvain Lemoine. Before Cambridge I completed an MSc in Human Evolution and Behaviour at UCL (Distinction) and a BA at UNC Chapel Hill, where I studied biology, psychology and chemistry.

I grew up around community mental health care, which is a big part of why I'm interested in evolutionary psychiatry. Outside research I've worked as an EMT and a surf instructor, and I'm originally from Wilmington, North Carolina.

Research

My work connects primatology with mental health through comparative cognition. Current themes:

Cognition and self-directed behaviour

Barbary macaques · Gibraltar

How macaques explore and manipulate mirrors and reflective objects, as part of my Cambridge PhD.

Inhibitory control

Japanese macaques · Koshima

A multi-site replication of the cylinder task, looking at individual variation across sex and rank.

Social bonds and trust

Chacma baboons · Tsaobis, Namibia

Whether grooming of vulnerable body parts reflects bond strength between partners.

Evolutionary psychiatry

Clinical & cross-cultural

How psychiatrists use evolutionary frameworks in practice, and how adolescence, cultural transmission and social isolation shape mental health across species.
