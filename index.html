01 — About

Field to clinic

I'm a PhD student in Biological Anthropology at Darwin College, Cambridge, supervised by Dr Sylvain Lemoine.

I study how primates think and how they build relationships, and what that comparative work can tell us about human mental health. I grew up around community mental health care, and that question has followed me into the field, from chimpanzees in western Tanzania to macaques in Japan and Gibraltar.

Before Cambridge I completed an MSc in Human Evolution and Behaviour at UCL (Distinction) and a BA at UNC Chapel Hill. I've also worked as an EMT and a surf instructor. Home is Wilmington, North Carolina.

How Barbary macaques explore and use mirrors and reflective surfaces. The core project of my PhD.

02

Inhibitory control

Koshima, Japan

A multi-site replication of the cylinder task with Japanese macaques, testing how self-control varies with sex and dominance rank.

03

Social bonds & trust

Tsaobis, Namibia

Whether grooming of vulnerable body regions reflects the strength of the bond between partners, drawing on 583 hours of video of wild chacma baboons.

04

Evolutionary psychiatry

UK & comparative

Focus groups with UK psychiatrists on how evolutionary frameworks are used in clinical practice, alongside comparative work on adolescence, cultural transmission and social isolation.
